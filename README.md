# On-chain Tactical Arena

A two-player on-chain tactical game in development. Each player commands three units on an 8x8 board, using hidden orders, simultaneous movement, combat, and objective control.

The current project provides a Vue/TypeScript local-account lobby, host-controlled room starts, a Phaser battlefield with browser Commit/Reveal rounds, a public match history and replay timeline, independent Go rules and hashing, deterministic offline simulation, PostgreSQL event indexing with chain reorganization recovery, independently verified historical replay APIs, and a recoverable Keeper for public progression. Arena remains authoritative for settlement and timeout handling. The browser saves orders before Commit, restores Reveal after refresh, supports encrypted order backups, continues confirmed rounds automatically, and provides manual timeout controls. History supports read-only inspection, round navigation, animation playback, and visible verification status. External wallet integration remains planned.

## Game design

The game uses a fixed Guardian/Ranger/Scout squad. Players commit to hidden orders, reveal their orders and random salts, and let Solidity resolve movement, combat, resources, and victory. There is no NFT, token, staking, or real-asset economy.

The [map configuration](config/map.json) contains the 8x8 Arena layout, mirrored starting slots, resources, central control cells, ASCII, and bitmaps. Coordinates start at the upper-left corner; `cellId = y * 8 + x`. Each player's three actions follow squad slot order. Slots remain fixed after death, and dead units contribute HOLD.

| Unit     |  HP | MOVE limit | ATTACK                                           | Special action                                                               |
| -------- | --: | ---------: | ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Guardian |  10 |     1 step | Orthogonally adjacent cell, damage 2             | SHIELD: 1 Energy; reduce this round's accumulated incoming damage by up to 2 |
| Ranger   |   6 |    2 steps | Straight horizontal/vertical range 1-3, damage 2 | POWER_SHOT: 2 Energy; same targeting rules, damage 3                         |
| Scout    |   5 |    3 steps | Orthogonally adjacent cell, damage 1             | DASH: 1 Energy; move up to 4 steps                                           |

Each live unit selects one action. Movement supplies a complete, nonempty orthogonal path, excluding the starting cell. Paths cannot cross obstacles, revisit a cell including their origin, or enter another live unit's starting cell. All moves sharing an endpoint fail; shared intermediate empty cells are allowed. HOLD means staying in place without attacking or spending Energy.

Attacks target cells within the attacker's range at round start. Empty targets are valid. Obstacles block Ranger rays; units do not. After movement, attacks hit only enemies occupying their target cells. Damage is simultaneous, including attacks from units killed that round, and HP cannot fall below zero. SHIELD reduces the Guardian's total accumulated damage once. Combined skill costs must fit the player's round-start Energy and remain spent when a move fails or a shot misses.

Players start with 0 Victory Points and 2 Energy, capped at 5 Energy. After deaths are removed, each occupied resource grants 1 Energy. The sole majority across the four central cells gains 2 Victory Points; equal counts gain none. Outcome priority is elimination, the 12-point threshold, then the 20-round limit. A sole surviving side wins; simultaneous elimination draws. At the point threshold or round limit, compare Victory Points, then surviving HP, then draw.

A waiting room lasts 24 hours. Its creator is the host. A different address may take the second seat at or before the room deadline, without starting the battle. Only the host can start a full room while it is still valid. Starting initializes the board, opens round 1, and starts the Commit timer. The host may cancel any room that has not started, including a full room; anyone may expire a waiting room strictly after the deadline. Commit and Reveal each have a 600-second window, opening the next phase as soon as both valid submissions arrive. Submissions are timely at the deadline; timeout finalization requires a later timestamp. One missing submission forfeits; two missing submissions draw, without executing a battle round. Anyone may finalize timeouts or settle a round with both valid reveals.

Commitments cannot be replaced. Invalid reveals can be retried before the deadline using the original committed data. Both valid reveals enter Resolvable, allowing anyone to settle that round once. Nonterminal settlement advances the round, clears old commitments and revealed orders, and opens a new Commit window. Terminal settlement preserves the final round and closes the match permanently. Match rules and map settings remain fixed throughout play.

## Architecture

- **Solidity / Foundry:** authoritative room and turn protocol storage, pure battle resolution, atomic settlement, and complete matches.
- **Go / PostgreSQL:** independent rules, encoding, hashing, simulation, transactional event indexing, automatic chain reorganization recovery, and read APIs for matches, historical replay, and verification. A dedicated Keeper handles public settlement, timeout finalization, and waiting-room expiry with persisted transaction recovery.
- **Vue 3 / TypeScript / Vite / Phaser:** a local-account lobby, live gameplay through RPC, persistent round secrets, one squad confirmation per round, confirmed result animations, and API-backed match history with a controllable replay timeline. External wallet signing remains planned.

The local browser sends transactions through Anvil's unlocked accounts, with signing performed by the node. The contract uses each transaction sender to identify its player. External wallet signing remains planned. Backend services provide convenience and verification; they cannot override contract state or match outcomes.

## Contract protocol

[Arena](contracts/src/Arena.sol) stores matches and exposes room operations, Commit/Reveal, public round settlement, timeout finalization, and queries. [GameTypes](contracts/src/GameTypes.sol) defines enums, action/state layouts, fixed rules, map data, and initial battle state. [GameHash](contracts/src/GameHash.sol) implements canonical encoding checks and hashing. [IArena](contracts/src/IArena.sol) declares the shared protocol signatures, events, and errors.

[TurnProtocol](contracts/src/TurnProtocol.sol) runs internally within Arena. Commit stores each player's immutable hash and submission flag; the second Commit opens a fresh Reveal window. Reveal checks the current player, round, deadline, complete commitment context, and squad legality before storing accepted actions and emitting their public payload. The second valid Reveal enters Resolvable with no phase deadline. Failed submissions revert their state and events. Anyone may finalize an expired Commit or Reveal phase: a sole submitter wins and bilateral absence draws. Timeout updates the terminal outcome, reason, and battle hash while preserving the board, resources, and round; it emits PhaseAdvanced and MatchFinished.

Settlement passes the accepted round-start state and both revealed squads to BattleResolver, then atomically stores the resulting battle, summary, and canonical state hash. RoundResolved identifies the settled round and carries the before/after hashes, resulting player resources, outcome, and reason. A continuing result already contains the next round number; TurnProtocol clears the previous inputs and emits PhaseAdvanced with a fresh 600-second Commit deadline. A terminal result emits PhaseAdvanced and MatchFinished with no deadline, retaining the final submission record. Finished matches reject further writes. MatchStarted and ActionRevealed expose the initial state and accepted orders/salts needed to reconstruct settlement results. For a timeout, replay retains the preceding board, resources, and round, applies the outcome/reason from MatchFinished, and verifies its state hash; that transition emits no RoundResolved.

[MapRules](contracts/src/MapRules.sol) implements coordinate conversion, bitmap queries, orthogonal paths, and wall-blocked straight rays. [UnitRules](contracts/src/UnitRules.sol) validates an entire three-action squad against the round-start snapshot and returns its total skill cost without changing state. It reuses the canonical encoding checks, enforces type-specific actions and ranges, blocks paths through live units' initial cells, requires dead slots to HOLD, and rejects Energy overspending. Shared empty endpoints remain valid during static validation. TurnProtocol uses these checks when accepting a Reveal, and BattleResolver reuses them before resolving movement conflicts.

[ResultRules](contracts/src/ResultRules.sol) evaluates a settlement candidate after damage, deaths, and scoring. Elimination takes priority over Victory Points, followed by the round limit. Points are compared before remaining total HP, with a draw on equality. Before a continuing round is advanced, the candidate's round identifies the round just resolved. The function returns an outcome and reason without changing the candidate, and preserves an existing terminal result, including protocol forfeits. BattleResolver uses it after world updates.

[BattleResolver](contracts/src/BattleResolver.sol) takes an ongoing battle snapshot and both action squads, validates both against the unchanged input, and produces an independent next state and ResolutionSummary. It deducts skill costs, cancels every move in a shared-endpoint group, resolves all target-cell attacks on the moved board, applies aggregate shielding and damage, removes deaths, awards resources/control, and evaluates the result. All attacks contribute before any HP is deducted. Continuing output states advance the round; terminal outputs retain the resolved round. The summary follows fixed player/unit slot order and records Energy spent, successful moves, actual HP lost, credited Energy after caps, and Victory Points gained. The input snapshot and action arrays remain unchanged.

Each action has a kind, target cell, path length, and four path cells. Unused targets are 255; unused path entries are zero. Dead units retain their type and slot, with zero HP and cell 255. Hashes use Keccak-256 over standard ABI encoding. Action commitments bind chain, contract, match, round, player, rules, orders, and salt; generate and retain secrets locally before broadcasting.

The battle hash covers match context, rules, map, round, player resources, all six units, and outcome. Commitments, salts, phase flags, and deadlines are excluded. An ongoing state's round is the next round to resolve; a terminal state retains its final round. Timeout preserves the board and resources while updating the outcome and reason.

The [shared state vectors](config/state-vectors.json) contain the frozen rules hash, result candidates with expected outcomes/reasons, and complete hash states with fixed contexts and expected digests. Result candidates use the round being evaluated; hash states already use the next round when play continues. Hash cases cover the initial state, chain/contract/match isolation, a continuing state, canonical death, terminal draws, and protocol timeouts. Expected digests were generated offline using Cast's standard ABI encoder and Keccak-256. Solidity, Go, and TypeScript can consume the same data without relying on a running backend.

The [shared battle vectors](config/battle-vectors.json) contain complete input snapshots, both action squads, explicit expected next states and summaries, and fixed expected battle hashes. They cover shared endpoints and paths, moving targets, empty/friendly targets, rays through units, aggregate shielding, simultaneous deaths, overkill, resource income/caps, control ties, victory priority, and the final round. Their expected states and summaries are authored per case, with digests generated offline using Cast. Central control awards points to only one side per round; comparisons with both players already at the threshold remain covered by the state vectors.

The default backend launch builds the contracts with the local compiler and deploys Arena with an empty room registry. Explicit demo and test modes generate real on-chain matches; ordinary startup waits for browser actions.

## Go rules and offline tools

The [Go rule package](server/internal/game/types.go) implements fixed state and action types, canonical validation, legal paths and targets, squad Energy budgets, simultaneous round resolution, outcomes, static ABI encoding, and protocol Keccak-256 hashing. It uses value copies of fixed arrays and has no RPC, wallet, database, or process dependency. The simulator and replay service share these rules. Go predictions and simulations have no authority over official on-chain games.

The [vector runner](server/internal/offline/vectors.go) reads the existing 18 outcome cases, 12 state hashes, and 20 battle cases. The [protocol vectors](config/protocol-vectors.json) add 13 fixed action-encoding, ActionsHash, and Commitment references generated offline with Viem's standard ABI encoder and Keccak-256. They cover all action kinds, context isolation, round and player binding, and Salt boundaries. Expected values are committed data and are not generated by the Go implementation during verification. Differences identify the case, field, expected value, and actual value.

Run the fixed-vector comparison from the project root:

```
./scripts/run-backend.sh verify
```

This mode compares Go with the fixed references, then runs the existing Solidity outcome and battle checks and the new protocol comparison inside Forge's offline EVM. It requires neither Anvil nor PostgreSQL. The backend script uses `go run -mod=mod` for offline commands and `go build -mod=mod` for normal startup. Go downloads missing build dependencies and maintains its dependency records as needed, reusing successfully cached downloads on subsequent runs. Routine startup does not run `go mod tidy`, which also resolves dependencies used by upstream tests and other platforms.

Run deterministic multi-round simulation and differential comparison:

```
./scripts/run-backend.sh simulate --seed 42 --games 10
```

The [generator](server/internal/game/generate.go) enumerates legal actions in a fixed order and uses an explicitly seeded random source. Each squad stays within its round-start Energy budget. Generated games run to a contract-rule terminal result, and the same seed and game count produce the same complete input/output records. Timing and throughput are reported separately from deterministic records. The [offline Solidity comparator](contracts/script/CompareRules.s.sol) independently resolves every generated input and compares all state fields, summary fields, and StateHash. It runs in a local EVM with no RPC or broadcast. A difference or Solidity rejection stops the command with the failing seed/case and a single-round reproduction containing its full start state, context, and both squads. To replay a saved failure, pass its printed reproduction filename through the simulation mode's `--input` option. Run records and reproduction files stay in the ignored local working area.

Both offline modes use the existing backend script and overwrite the same root log while displaying output in the terminal. They run before chain startup logic, so selecting one does not start or deploy a chain, clear the published deployment, or create game transactions. The default backend mode continues to prepare the local chain and wait for browser operations; it does not run batch simulation. The first dependency preparation may need network access; the rule calculations and Solidity reference execution are offline once tools and dependencies are available. Runtime comparison results and performance baselines must be established by the maintainer when invoking these modes.

## Local account lobby

The [lobby](client/src/views/LobbyView.vue) creates rooms using the selected local account and opens existing rooms by ID or a copied invitation URL from the same application. Copy invitation produces a link that can be pasted into Find a room or opened directly in another tab. Creation obtains the actual ID from the confirmed MatchCreated event. The [room view](client/src/views/RoomView.vue) reads players, phase, round, deadlines, and result directly from Arena. A different local account can take the open seat in a waiting room. Both accounts remain in the preparation room until its host presses Start game. The contract rejects starts by other accounts, starts without an opponent, full-room joins, repeated starts, and starts after the room deadline. The room page refreshes contract getters from live update notifications, falls back to one-second checks when updates or reads fail, and automatically takes its seated accounts to the battlefield after a confirmed start, including when an already-started invitation is reopened.

The account bar reads unlocked accounts and balances from Anvil through Viem's [JSON-RPC wallet client](https://viem.sh/docs/clients/wallet). The page stores the selected address in session storage, independently for each browser tab. Account switching is available in the lobby and clears form input/errors, invitation feedback, and transaction notices. Preparation and battlefield headers show a read-only account name and shortened address; use Lobby to change accounts. A directly opened invitation remains on its room page during initial account loading. Room roles follow the creator and opponent recorded by the contract. The node signs transactions, and the browser displays their pending, confirmed, or failed status. Deployment changes clear the transaction context and refresh the current room.

The [synchronization helper](scripts/client-contract.mjs) exports the [typed ABI](client/src/generated/arena.ts) from the compiler artifact. After Arena is deployed, the backend publishes public connection information for the browser and removes it when its node stops. Deployment timestamps distinguish fresh local sessions when the deterministic Arena address repeats. The frontend supplies connection feedback, inline room-ID errors, retry actions, disabled submission states, and responsive layouts. Live gameplay uses RPC without a Go API dependency, wallet extension, private-key import, or manually entered deployment address. Historical replay uses the Go read API.

## Battlefield and action planning

The preparation room keeps Start game visible to its host from creation onward. It is disabled until an opponent joins, and joining only takes the second seat. The host explicitly starts the game; the contract then initializes the battlefield and first Commit deadline. Both room pages automatically enter the battle after confirmation. The browser checks the MatchStarted transaction sender against the room host before entering, so an older local deployment that starts on join produces an actionable restart message. A direct battle link before the host starts returns to the preparation room.

The [battlefield view](client/src/views/BattleView.vue) owns a dedicated game layout with a compact account and room header, player resources, the board, and three order summaries. Its layout is independent of the lobby shell. The [Phaser renderer](client/src/battlefield/BattlefieldScene.ts) draws confirmed terrain and units from the chain. Vue receives tile selections through the [typed bridge](client/src/battlefield/bridge.ts). The board uses one fixed logical coordinate system; Phaser fits its canvas inside a stable container, and keyboard tile controls align to the actual canvas bounds. Opening or closing unit details never changes that container.

Click a unit to open its [side drawer](client/src/components/UnitActionDrawer.vue). Player A units always open it on the left; Player B units always open it on the right, following their spawn side even after movement. It shows confirmed stats, the current local order, and actions with their Energy costs. The drawer is compact, capped below a quarter of the game viewport width, and bounded by its height. A dark backdrop dims the rest of the game and blocks background interaction. The close button, backdrop, and Escape dismiss it. Native modal focus stays within the drawer while it is open. Opponent and defeated units expose information only.

Choose Move or Dash to close the drawer, remove the backdrop, and highlight reachable destinations. Clicking a destination previews a shortest legal path; Attack and Power shot highlight legal target tiles instead. The target controls occupy a reserved area below the board. Confirm replaces the selected unit's local draft after geometry and shared Energy checks pass, then reopens its drawer. For Move and Dash, the full piece moves to the saved destination as a pink DRAFT preview while a faint START marker remains at its confirmed origin. An unconfirmed target remains a planning outline. Both the preview piece and its start marker can be selected. Shared draft destinations show smaller stacked pieces; repeated tile selections cycle through them. Cancel reopens the drawer with its original draft. Hold and Shield can be reviewed and confirmed within the drawer. Closing details retains saved drafts; Reset to Hold clears one unit's draft, and Undo restores the previous saved order and its displayed position. Defeated slots retain Hold.

The [planning helpers](client/src/lib/battle.ts) follow round-start path geometry, occupancy, wall blocking, range, skills, and Energy rules. Empty and friendly attack targets remain legal. Moves may share intermediate empty tiles; identical destinations remain legal orders even though those moves fail at settlement. Valid bundles use three actions in squad slot order, target 255 where unused, and zero-padded four-cell paths. Local previews do not calculate authoritative movement, damage, income, or victory.

The [battle store](client/src/stores/battle.ts) keeps confirmed snapshots separate from saved drafts and the pending selection. All battlefield getters are pinned to one block. Team-colored units and resources show chain data; pink pieces, paths, and target markers show local plans. Displayed draft positions are separate from the chain snapshot: movement and range validation still use round-start positions, and previews never change HP, Energy, or Victory Points. Uncommitted drafts remain in memory. Commit saves the complete squad and Salt in IndexedDB before broadcasting. Saved orders are isolated by deployment session, chain, Arena, room, round, and account; refreshing or returning to the selected account restores them. Phase changes preserve the original orders for Reveal, while a new round starts with Hold drafts. A failed Commit retains the saved orders and Salt for retry. Pre-Reveal orders and Salt stay out of application APIs, logs, and telemetry.

## Browser round controls

Unit Confirm saves one local order. **Confirm orders** submits the entire three-unit squad. A compact modal explains that the orders will be locked for this round, with **Confirm**, **Keep editing**, and **Don't show this again**. Closing the modal, pressing Escape, or clicking its backdrop returns to editing. The preference is stored per local account in this browser. It skips future reminders; each round still requires its own Confirm orders click.

Confirmation generates a random 32-byte Salt, computes the standard-ABI commitment matching Solidity, and waits for durable IndexedDB transaction completion before sending it. The page waits for both confirmations, reveals each account's original saved orders automatically, and settles after both Reveals are accepted. The account that revealed last can send settlement; the Keeper can also settle a resolvable round. If another caller advances the room first, the browser refreshes the chain state without presenting that completed operation as a failure. The contract updates the board, HP, Energy, Victory Points, round, and result. Room starts remain host-controlled. A new round waits for another explicit squad confirmation.

Keep the participating pages open while a round is processed. Refresh or returning to the account restores its saved secret and resumes the confirmed round. Unsent saved orders use Retry confirmation; failed automatic requests pause and expose Retry. Processing uses on-chain flags and phase state before sending another transaction. Closing a page pauses its participation until it is reopened.

Use **Orders backup** to export the saved round as a password-encrypted file. Encryption uses browser Web Crypto with PBKDF2-SHA256 and AES-GCM. Keep the file and password until the round is complete. Import checks deployment, chain, Arena, room, round, account, rules, canonical orders, and the on-chain commitment when one exists. Another browser connected to the same local deployment can restore the original orders. IndexedDB survives refresh but is not an encrypted vault; clearing site data removes local orders. If the original orders and backup are both lost, the Salt cannot be reconstructed.

The [Vite configuration](client/vite.config.js) sends a Content Security Policy header in development and local preview. Scripts load only from the application origin; inline scripts and string evaluation are blocked. Connections allow the application origin and local RPC, with the development WebSocket allowed only during development. Inline styles support Vue bindings and Vite style updates, and data images support Phaser's built-in textures. Encrypted backup downloads remain available. Embedding remains available for the editor's browser. These headers apply to Vite's servers; a separate production host must supply the same policy itself.

The page shows the chain's UTC phase deadline and a countdown using local time. Anvil retains transaction-triggered mining. When time has elapsed, **Finalize timeout** appears. On that click, the browser refreshes the phase; if the latest block time is still within the deadline, it updates the next local block to the current time and mines one block. It then reads the confirmed state and sends timeout finalization only when the chain timestamp is strictly beyond the same phase deadline. The contract decides the forfeit or draw. The Keeper also finalizes expired phases and waiting rooms. On idle Anvil it may perform the same on-demand synchronization after an actual deadline has passed. Ordinary refresh does not mine blocks, and interval mining remains disabled. Submissions at the exact deadline remain valid.

For newly observed RoundResolved events, the renderer reads battle states immediately before and at the event block. Confirmed surviving movements follow their revealed paths; HP loss, defeated-piece removal, Energy and point changes, and results use confirmed state. Pink drafts stay separate from these results. A defeated unit's final cell is erased by the contract, so its removal does not invent a movement result. Timeout shows the terminal result with the existing board. Reduced-motion preferences show the confirmed result directly. Refresh loads the current state without replaying past rounds.

## Public progression

The [Keeper](server/internal/indexer/keeper.go) runs inside the Go backend after successful indexing. It considers resolvable rounds, expired Commit/Reveal phases, and expired waiting rooms. Before broadcasting it rereads the actual contract phase, round, and deadline and simulates the public call. Only Solidity changes the battle outcome. Host starts and player orders remain under player control; Keeper never reads browser secrets or submits Commit/Reveal.

`KEEPER_ENABLED` defaults to `true`. Set it to `false` in the local backend environment before starting to exercise manual progression. The backend still provides indexing and read APIs. `KEEPER_PRIVATE_KEY` may select a dedicated local execution signer; leaving it empty uses a public development-only key. Go funds that local address through Anvil when its balance is zero. It remains separate from the two unlocked browser accounts. Keep custom private keys in the backend environment; no key is written to transaction records, published deployment data, or logs. Public-testnet configuration is outside the current local launcher.

Jobs bind deployment, match, round, phase, and operation. The [transaction manager](server/internal/indexer/keeper_tx.go) stores the signed transaction, nonce, fees, and hash before its first broadcast. It reconciles receipts across all replacement hashes on restart, resends missing transactions, and raises both fee caps for a pending transaction after 30 seconds using the same nonce. When a player has already completed a task, it skips an unsent operation; a stale pending transaction can be replaced by a zero-value self-transfer to release its nonce. Canonical receipt checks and indexed block identities reopen work displaced by reorganization. One in-flight nonce is handled at a time.

The startup output reports the dedicated address or an actionable Keeper failure; `/sync` includes its enabled state, address, and current error. RPC/database failures pause new submissions, while player RPC operations remain independent. Logs report submissions, replacements, receipts, and changed failure/recovery states without idle scan messages. Normal local time synchronization never jumps ahead of wall time or mines periodic empty blocks.

During scripted history and branch acceptance, a PostgreSQL advisory lock pauses Keeper work while leaving indexing and the read API active. Keeper-specific scenarios release that lock for actual background processing and hold it again for controlled faults. The connection releases its lock when the acceptance process exits. Keep manual matches idle while fault acceptance is running.

The selected frame is retained in the URL. A frame labels the round that was settled; its resulting battle state can already contain the next round number. Clicking a unit opens a narrow read-only drawer on its birth side. Round details expose public orders and settlement effects. Verification details expose hashes, source references, and a first-difference link. The replay API supplies the frozen map and rules, with map bitmaps encoded as decimal strings for lossless browser conversion.

History and replay use the [API adapter](client/src/lib/history.ts) and a [separate store](client/src/stores/history.ts). Vite forwards same-origin API requests to the configured Go service in development and preview. Replay never starts live order submission. History uses live notifications while mounted, falls back to four-second checks when updates are interrupted, cancels requests on navigation, and identifies data by chain, Arena, deployment hash, and match ID. Changed branches pause and reload the timeline. Removed matches lose their old picture and verification. Connection failures clear the current Verified badge; a retained picture is marked unavailable until rechecked. API failures do not disable the live RPC game flow. Account changes do not turn public history into another account's planning session.

The [Indexer](server/internal/indexer/chain.go) uses go-ethereum block-range log queries and batched header reads, ordered by block height and log index. It preserves raw logs, decoded payloads, block/parent hashes, transaction hashes, and log positions. One PostgreSQL transaction saves each range's headers, events, projections, and cursor. Duplicate logs skip projection. The [schema](server/internal/indexer/schema.sql) creates deployment, block, event, match, round, public action, snapshot, cursor, Keeper-job, transaction-attempt, and durable stream-notification tables. Keeper records reference the deployment and survive projection rebuilds and branch rollback.

The [Projector](server/internal/indexer/project.go) follows public events and uses the Go rules to build round snapshots. Pre-Reveal orders and salts never enter the database. Stored snapshots remain projections; verification separately reconstructs the history from current canonical RPC logs and compares those projections with the independently computed results.

On a changed cursor hash or broken parent link, [branch recovery](server/internal/indexer/reorg.go) searches backward for the common ancestor. One database transaction archives displaced blocks and their original events, removes affected projections, reconstructs affected matches from surviving logs, and moves the cursor back. Indexing then processes replacement blocks. Archived branches do not appear in match, event, or replay queries. Startup performs the same recovery when the chain changed while Go was stopped. A deployment block that has itself left the chain requires restarting the backend with the current deployment.

The [read API](server/internal/indexer/http.go) defaults to http://127.0.0.1:8080:

| GET endpoint                          | Result                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/events`                             | Committed update notifications over SSE, with resumption and reset                                        |
| `/health`                             | Database, RPC, Indexer, Keeper, and event-stream readiness                                                |
| `/ready`                              | Initial indexing complete, no known sync error, and no observed lag                                       |
| `/sync`                               | Active deployment, observed tip, indexed cursor/hash, confirmation policy, sync errors, and record counts |
| `/matches?limit=20&offset=0`          | Match page, ordered by numeric match ID                                                                   |
| `/matches/1`                          | Match detail, projected battle state, and source block/transaction/log                                    |
| `/matches/1/events?limit=20&offset=0` | Raw and decoded public events, ordered by block/log position                                              |
| `/matches/1/replay?limit=20&offset=0` | Reconstructed frame page, public round inputs, and independent verification                               |
| `/matches/1/verification`             | Verification status, checked cursor, completed-round count, and first difference                          |
| `/matches/1/snapshots?round=1`        | Reconstructed snapshots for a settled round, including the initial frame for round 1                      |

Pagination accepts limits from 1 to 100 and nonnegative offsets; `hasMore` indicates another page. Data and cursor metadata are read from one consistent database transaction. A zero confirmation depth labels local observations `observed`. A positive depth labels observations `provisional` until enough descendant blocks exist, then `confirmed`. These labels do not claim network finality or independent Replay verification. No extra blocks are mined to satisfy confirmation depth. A known RPC/indexing error or branch recovery makes confirmation status `unknown` and readiness fail while existing records remain readable. The browser continues using RPC directly.

The [replay engine](server/internal/indexer/replay.go) starts with MatchStarted and consumes public commitments and Reveal events in chain order. It checks the initial state, recomputes commitments, validates actions, resolves each round with the independent Go rules, and compares the resulting hashes, resources, and outcome with chain events. Timeout replay derives the result from the accepted submissions while retaining the preceding board. Verification also compares stored raw logs, payloads, rounds, public actions, snapshots, and match details with that reconstruction. Frames include the settlement's round, the resulting state's round, source block/transaction/log, chain and computed hashes, and resolution effects.

`Verified` means a finished battle's complete history, independent computation, and indexed records agree with the current canonical chain through the reported cursor. `Mismatch` identifies the first affected round, field, and expected/actual values. `Incomplete` covers missing history, unavailable canonical evidence, recovery, and unfinished battles. A consistent unfinished prefix sets `prefixVerified` and reports its verified-round count; waiting or cancelled rooms have no battle to replay. Verification runs on each request and pins the deployment and cursor before and after reading RPC history. No persisted Verified flag or stored snapshot substitutes for current chain evidence. Verification and block-depth confirmation are separate properties, displayed separately in the replay page. Replay and snapshot pages return computed states; an overall Mismatch still identifies changes in the stored projections.

## Live updates and service recovery

The [event stream](server/internal/indexer/stream.go) exposes `GET /events` through the existing same-origin API proxy. Each committed indexing range appends one notification containing its source cursor and affected match IDs in the same PostgreSQL transaction as the canonical projections. Duplicate rescans produce no additional range notification. No browser orders, secrets, or authoritative battle state enter this channel. Clients reread RPC getters or history APIs after a notification; independent replay still determines verification.

IDs bind the active deployment and a monotonic database sequence. `Last-Event-ID` resumes after the last received notification; an invalid cursor or another deployment receives `reset` and the current cursor. Notifications survive Go restarts, projection rebuilding, and chain rollback. Branch recovery commits a `reorg` notice before replacement-range notifications; reindexing commits a `reset` notice. Clients must discard affected transient state and reread current data after either event. Historical notification payloads describe source changes at their recorded cursor, rather than certifying that an old branch is still valid. The stream flushes messages immediately, sends status heartbeats, bounds each read batch, and releases queries and connections when the client leaves. Its per-write deadline is renewed for heartbeats rather than inheriting the ordinary HTTP response timeout.

The [shared browser connection](client/src/stores/updates.ts) opens one native EventSource per tab, deduplicates event IDs, batches normal changes, and reconnects automatically. If an HTTP error closes EventSource permanently, the page creates a replacement after three seconds and passes its last event ID through the supported `after` query parameter. Stopping the connection cancels pending retries. The shared development and preview API proxy closes the browser's event stream when its upstream response aborts or errors, so a killed Go process cannot leave an apparently connected browser stream behind. History clears previous verification and cancels affected playback during recovery. A reorg removes the old timeline before reading replacement data. Room and battle views refresh only relevant games and retain direct RPC fallback. Live RPC snapshots also check their previous source block to reject orphaned animations when push delivery is unavailable. Persisted orders and Salt stay in IndexedDB and are checked against the current on-chain commitment before Reveal resumes. No new round is confirmed automatically.

History displays push connection status separately from Indexer and block confirmation state. Live room and battle headers show a small recovery message only while updates are interrupted. Reconnection triggers fresh reads; an unavailable stream can coexist with functioning RPC gameplay and successful history HTTP reads. A cached picture alone cannot restore Verified.

`/health` distinguishes database availability, RPC availability, Indexer synchronization/recovery, event delivery storage, and Keeper state. `/ready` fails while the known chain tip is ahead of the completed sync or the Indexer has a known source error. The backend identifies its PostgreSQL sessions and reacquires the deployment's advisory lock after a lost database connection. The existing launcher restarts unexpectedly terminated Go services without discarding the managed Anvil chain; readiness is checked again after recovery. Repeated failures are reported and stop the launcher. Explicit Ctrl+C still closes services owned by that launcher.

The [stream acceptance scenarios](server/internal/indexer/check_stream.go) run through the existing backend test suffix. They check uncommitted and rolled-back notification invisibility, identical IDs and payloads after reconnect, invalid-cursor reset, continued RPC writes during an unrequested Go SIGKILL, RPC suspension and recovery, project database connection termination, advisory-lock reacquisition, and multi-block replacement branches. They require old-branch victories to disappear and withdrawal IDs to precede replacement IDs. Recovery timings and received event IDs join the local acceptance report. Controlled chain changes are restored afterward.

Browser recovery then checks the rendered application without clicking Refresh or reloading the document. Interrupting only the SSE connection must display reconnection feedback while ordinary HTTP polling keeps the history list current. Restoring SSE must reconnect automatically. A deliberate Go SIGKILL must clear current verification and recover the open replay after the launcher restarts Go. Real contract transactions must update the page, and a real replacement branch must remove the old winning replay and its animation before the replacement read finishes. The replacement room must show its own incomplete history without retaining the old victory.

The database-service scenario is separate from the earlier project-session interruption. It sends PostgreSQL's fast-shutdown signal to `system/postgresql-16`, waits for the backend and page to report the outage, and creates a room through the still-running RPC. It then starts the same service, checks that the PostgreSQL start time and Indexer lock owner changed, requires Go to retain its process, and verifies that indexing catches up without changing earlier records. The open page must obtain fresh verification automatically. No database contents are deleted. The driver attempts to restart PostgreSQL on failure or interruption, restores its chain snapshot and canonical records, and closes its temporary browser and frontend. PostgreSQL remains running after the checks; cleanup failures are reported explicitly with a recovery command when necessary.

The browser driver saves a separate local recovery report with timings, received event IDs, browser stream completion/failure events, and database restart evidence. The Go crash scenario requires the proxy to close the browser stream, the page to invalidate current verification, and recovery to fetch fresh history without reloading the document. A browser failure also saves a screenshot when the browser is available and the database does not need immediate recovery. Only an overall pass after both backend and browser checks means the entire test command succeeded.

For simple UI inspection, start the backend normally, then the frontend. Use two tabs for one host-started round and confirm normal progression. For prepared replay data, use the existing demo suffix, open History, play a replay, and request a Go-only restart from another terminal through the backend entry. Check reconnection feedback, restored history, and verification after a fresh read. These short manual checks do not require finishing a match.
